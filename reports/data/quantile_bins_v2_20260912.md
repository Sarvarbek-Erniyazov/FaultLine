## The tails: 4 fixed-width bins a side

Every channel gets 4 fixed-width bins below the training p0.5% and 4 above the p99.5%, after the point masses take their exact bins; the rest of the bin budget is quantiles over the middle. The outermost bin on each side is then merged inward until it holds 0.1% of the channel's measured training values (ADR-0015), so a tail can end with fewer bins than it was laid with.

Every column is the same bin count fitted on the same values, so the fits differ by the tail rule and nothing else. `quantile only` is the M1b rule; a fit between it and `chosen` is a revision this one supersedes.

**What the floor costs, and it is not small.** On 2 channels the bottom bin and on 8 the top bin came out wider than the `quantile only` control -- wider than doing nothing, on the measure ADR-0014 was accepted for. The mechanism is arithmetic rather than a defect. A tail's values are packed against its inner boundary, so the outer fixed-width bins are nearly empty and the merge cascades; where it runs to the boundary the clamp bin spans the whole tail and holds 0.5% of the channel's values by construction, against the 0.39% a pure quantile end bin holds. A population floor and a fixed-width tail pull opposite ways and the floor wins. The two objectives of ADR-0015 cannot both be met by merging alone: this fit buys a clamp token the model can learn and pays for it in clamp resolution on most channels. Reported, not iterated on -- and the reason the tails are 1% of the values wide in the first place is the tail rule itself, so the honest reading is that the rule and the floor need one design, not two.
